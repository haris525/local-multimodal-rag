# global-3

Question: The original 1787 Constitution text uses the phrase "two thirds" (once spelled "two-thirds") several times. How many times does it occur, and for each occurrence: two thirds of whom, needed for what?

Gold answer: Nine times. Article I, Section 3: no one is convicted on impeachment without the concurrence of two thirds of the members (Senators) present. Article I, Section 5: each House may expel a member with the concurrence of two thirds. Article I, Section 7 (twice): a bill returned with the President's objections becomes law if two thirds of the House where it originated and then two thirds of the other House approve it; and (a third time) an order, resolution or vote needing both Houses that the President disapproves must be repassed by two thirds of the Senate and House. Article II, Section 1: when the House chooses the President, a quorum is a member or members from two thirds of the States. Article II, Section 2: treaties need two thirds of the Senators present. Article V (twice): Congress proposes amendments whenever two thirds of both Houses deem it necessary, and calls a convention for proposing amendments on the application of the legislatures of two thirds of the States.

## Arithmetic and notes

A case-insensitive search for "thirds" finds exactly nine lines: 81, 108, 145, 148, 162, 311, 357, 470 and 472 (each line holds one occurrence). "one third" (line 59, Senate classes) and "three fourths" (Article V, ratification) are other fractions and do not count; the text never writes 2/3. Counting the bill override once gives eight distinct requirements; the question asks for occurrences, which is nine.

## What makes it global

The occurrences are spread over five Sections in three Articles, about 400 lines apart, and each sits in a different topic (trials, discipline, lawmaking, presidential election, treaties, amendment). A retrieval of a handful of chunks returns the well-known ones and misses the quorum, the orders and resolutions, or the convention.
