# global-15

Question: Each of these three papers reports some human judgement of model outputs. For each paper, say who judged what, and give the numbers it reports for that human judgement.

Gold answer: Ragas: two annotators, fluent in English, labelled all questions of the new WikiEval dataset (built from 50 Wikipedia pages) along faithfulness, answer relevance and context relevance; they agreed in about 95% of cases for faithfulness and context relevance and about 90% for answer relevance, and Ragas agreed with the annotators 0.95, 0.78 and 0.70 of the time (Table 1). Mistral 7B: an independent human evaluation on llmboxing.com, where people picked the preferred of two anonymous answers; as of 6 October 2023 Mistral 7B's outputs were preferred 5020 times against 4143 times for Llama 2 13B. Seven Failure Points: the authors manually inspected 40 issues plus all answers that OpenAI's OpenEvals flagged as inaccurate in the BioASQ case study, and found the automated evaluation more pessimistic than a human rater (with the caveat that the reviewers were not domain experts).

Documents: ragas-rag-evaluation-2023.pdf, mistral-7b-2023.pdf, rag-seven-failure-points-2024.pdf

## Arithmetic and notes

Searches for annotator, human, manual and preferred in each paper find no other human judgement: Mistral's Chatbot Arena ELO in Table 3 is an external leaderboard, and the Seven Failure Points introduction (p2) describes the same manual inspection without numbers. "cov- ering", "con- sidered", "con- text" and "gener- ated" are the PDFs' line-break hyphens; "WikEval" is the paper's typo.

## What makes it global

One item per paper, each in a section about something else (dataset construction, instruction fine-tuning, a case study), with no shared vocabulary; a question-level retrieval over three documents tends to return the Ragas passage and miss one of the other two.
