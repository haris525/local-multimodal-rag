# global-2

Question: List every provision of the original 1787 Constitution text that names the Vice President, with its Article and Section, and say what each one provides.

Gold answer: The text names the Vice President in three Sections. Article I, Section 3: the Vice President is President of the Senate but has no vote unless the Senate is equally divided; the Senate chooses a President pro tempore in the Vice President's absence or when he exercises the office of President. Article II, Section 1: the Vice President is chosen for the same four-year term as the President; after the President is chosen, the person with the greatest number of electoral votes becomes Vice President, and if two or more remain with equal votes the Senate chooses the Vice President from them by ballot; if the President is removed, dies, resigns or cannot discharge the office, its powers and duties devolve on the Vice President, and Congress may by law declare which officer acts as President when both the President and the Vice President are gone. Article II, Section 4: the President, Vice President and all civil officers are removed from office on impeachment for, and conviction of, treason, bribery or other high crimes and misdemeanors. (The name appears eight times: twice as 'Vice-President', six times as 'Vice President'.)

## Evidence

- President of the Senate, no vote unless equally divided (Article I, Section 3; lines 70-71)
  - us-constitution-1787.txt (plain text, no pages)
  - "The Vice-President of the United States shall be President of the Senate, but shall have no Vote, unless they be equally divided."

- President pro tempore in his absence (Article I, Section 3; lines 73-75)
  - us-constitution-1787.txt (plain text, no pages)
  - "The Senate shall choose their other Officers, and also a President pro tempore, in the Absence of the Vice-President, or when he shall exercise the Office of President of the United States."

- chosen for the same four-year term (Article II, Section 1; lines 282-285)
  - us-constitution-1787.txt (plain text, no pages)
  - "He shall hold his Office during the Term of four Years, and, together with the Vice President chosen for the same Term, be elected, as follows:"

- runner-up becomes Vice President; the Senate breaks a tie by ballot (Article II, Section 1; lines 312-316)
  - us-constitution-1787.txt (plain text, no pages)
  - "In every Case, after the Choice of the President, the Person having the greatest Number of Votes of the Electors shall be the Vice President. But if there should remain two or more who have equal Votes, the Senate shall chuse from them by Ballot the Vice President."

- powers devolve on the Vice President; Congress provides for both offices (Article II, Section 1; lines 328-334)
  - us-constitution-1787.txt (plain text, no pages)
  - "In Case of the Removal of the President from Office, or of his Death, Resignation, or Inability to discharge the Powers and Duties of the said Office, the Same shall devolve on the Vice President, and the Congress may by Law provide for the Case of Removal, Death, Resignation or Inability, both of the President and Vice President, declaring what Officer shall then act as President,"

- removal on impeachment (Article II, Section 4; lines 381-383)
  - us-constitution-1787.txt (plain text, no pages)
  - "The President, Vice President and all civil Officers of the United States, shall be removed from Office on Impeachment for, and Conviction of, Treason, Bribery, or other high Crimes and Misdemeanors."

## Arithmetic and notes

A full-text search for "Vice" (excluding Service and Advice) finds exactly eight occurrences: lines 70 and 74 ("Vice-President") and lines 284, 314, 316, 330, 332 and 381 ("Vice President"). Line 299 has the "President of the Senate" opening the electoral certificates, which by Article I, Section 3 is the Vice President, but the text does not name him there; an answer that adds it is not wrong.

## What makes it global

The provisions sit in Article I, Section 3 (the Senate), in two different paragraphs of Article II, Section 1 (election and succession) and in Article II, Section 4 (impeachment). A partial answer typically has the Senate role and succession but misses the term, the runner-up election, the Senate tie-break or removal on impeachment.
